📖 About

My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, statistics, and cybersecurity. I've spent the past several years pulling these three fields closer together — moving from detection engineering, to supervised ML for alert triage, to building frontier-LLM-powered AI agents for incident response. Each step has reinforced the same conviction: the future of defense isn't going to be written in detection rules alone, it's going to be written in models.

What makes this moment urgent is the asymmetry between attackers and defenders. LLMs have changed the speed and sophistication of offense. Phishing is now hyper-personalized, social engineering scales infinitely, and reconnaissance happens at machine speed. The old assumption — that defenders have time to investigate because attackers move slowly — is gone. The only viable response is to meet machine-speed attacks with machine-speed defense: AI agents that triage, investigate, and act in seconds, not hours.

I'm especially interested in applying AI, LLMs, and ML to the Security Operations Center (SOC). SOCs sit at the front line of enterprise defense, drowning in alerts, repetitive triage, and false positives. They are also the layer of the security stack where AI delivers the most leverage — by classifying alerts, summarizing incident context, recommending analyst actions, and freeing humans for the investigations that actually require judgment. At Nike, I've built and shipped exactly this kind of tooling: a Decision Tree classifier that reduced manual DLP triage by ~30%, an LLM-based data labeling pipeline that eliminated manual annotation, and an AI agent that retrieves historical incident context and recommends next actions in real time.

I write here about what I learn building these systems — what works in production, what doesn't, and where applied ML in cybersecurity is going next.
